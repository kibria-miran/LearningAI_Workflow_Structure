CHECKPOINT 2: Understand What RAG Is
----------------------------------------------------------------

CONCEPT:
  RAG = Retrieval-Augmented Generation.

  The problem it solves: LLMs have a knowledge cutoff and no
  access to YOUR private documents. If you ask "What is our
  refund policy?", the model doesn't know — it's not in its
  training data.

  RAG fixes this by:
  1. Storing your documents as searchable chunks
  2. When a question arrives, finding the most relevant chunks
  3. Injecting those chunks into the prompt as context
  4. The model answers using ONLY that context

  Flow:
    Question -> Embed question -> Search index -> Top-k chunks
    -> Inject into prompt -> Model answers -> Cite sources

ANALOGY:
  Without RAG: asking a new employee a question about company
  policy. They guess based on general knowledge.

  With RAG: the employee has a searchable filing cabinet of
  every company document. They look up the answer before replying.

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  RAG is the most commercially deployed LLM pattern today.
  Every "chat with your documents" product uses it.
  Knowing how to build it from scratch is a core skill.

CHECKPOINT 5: Understand Embeddings at a High Level
----------------------------------------------------------------

CONCEPT:
  An embedding is a list of numbers (a vector) that represents
  the meaning of a piece of text.

  Similar meaning -> similar vectors -> close together in space.
  Different meaning -> different vectors -> far apart.

  Example:
    "How do I return a product?" -> [0.12, -0.45, 0.88, ...]
    "What is your refund policy?" -> [0.11, -0.43, 0.90, ...]
    "What is the capital of France?" -> [-0.72, 0.31, -0.15, ...]

  The first two are semantically similar -> their vectors are close.
  The third is unrelated -> its vector is far from the others.

  Cosine similarity measures how close two vectors are.
  Score of 1.0 = identical meaning. Score of 0.0 = unrelated.

ANALOGY:
  Imagine a map where every sentence is a dot. Sentences with
  similar meaning are placed near each other. RAG finds the dots
  nearest to your question and retrieves those sentences.
